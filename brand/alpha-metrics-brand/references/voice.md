# Voice and numbers

## Who we sound like

A careful senior researcher explaining results to a busy manager: direct, specific, friendly, and honest
about limits. The website's headline sums it up: *"Market research that says what the sample can carry."*

- **Plain words.** "We tell you what moves a metric, and what the data cannot show." Not "unlock
  actionable insights" or "leverage AI-powered synergies".
- **Specific over grand.** Name the measure, the market, the base. "Aided awareness in Austria rose from
  38% to 46%" beats "awareness grew strongly".
- **Claims sized to the evidence.** If a difference is inside the confidence interval, say it is not
  distinguishable. Never call a correlation an effect.
- **Short sentences, active voice, "we" and "you".**
- **British spelling**: analyse, colour, programme, optimise, behaviour, centre. Dates as 12 November 2026.
- **Sentence case** in headings and titles. No exclamation marks. No emoji in documents or slides.
- Words to avoid: revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, unlock, leverage, seamless, robust (unless
  statistical), deep dive, synergy, "AI-powered" as a selling point.
- German texts follow the same voice; use "Sie", and keep the brand name in lowercase:
  alpha-metrics.ai.

## Reporting numbers

- Every sample-based figure carries its **base**: "Base: all respondents, Austria, n = 1,003."
- Give intervals where decisions depend on them: "46% (95% CI 43–49%)" or "46% ± 3.1 pp".
- Percentages: no decimals below n ≈ 1,000 unless needed; one decimal at most. Changes in **pp**
  (percentage points), with a real minus sign: −4 pp, +6 pp.
- Thousands separator: comma in English (1,003), space or point in German (1 003 / 1.003).
- Name the question in plain words the first time ("recognise the brand when shown its name": aided awareness).
- Synthetic or illustrative data is always labelled: "Fictional brand, synthetic data."
- Sources and fieldwork dates go in a grey line under the chart or table, never hidden in an appendix only.

## Structures that work

**Slide or chart title** = the takeaway as a full sentence; the subtitle = what was measured.

**Report**: summary with the 3–5 findings first (each one sentence + the number), then method in one
paragraph (sample, field period, weighting, margin of error), then findings by theme, then implications, then
appendix.

**Blog post / LinkedIn**: a concrete hook (a number or a common mistake), the explanation, one chart, a
practical takeaway. 600–1,000 words for the blog; 120–200 words for LinkedIn with one image.

**Boilerplate** (about us): "alpha-metrics.ai is a market research consultancy in Vienna. We design, field
and analyse brand and consumer studies for companies in Austria and the DACH region. Every number comes with
its confidence interval, its base and its limits, so you can decide on it."
